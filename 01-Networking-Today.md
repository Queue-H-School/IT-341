
# LECTURE 1: EXPLORING THE NETWORK #

<u>Peer to peer network</u></br>
\- not centralized, not scalable, slow</br>
\- print something, very small (home) office setup</br>

<u>Devices</u></br>
\- host/server, returns and holds information</br>
\- clinet/end device,  makes requests</br>
\- intermediary, management and error information</br>

<u>Wires</u></br>
\- Metal wires, use electrical impulses</br>
\- Fiber optic, physical pulses of light (faster)</br>
\- modulation of specific EM frequencies</br>

Network Interface Card: Connects a device to the network</br>
Port/Interface: A specific endpoint within a device</br>

PAN (personal) \-\-> **LAN (local) \-\-> WAN (wide, provided by ISPs)** \-\-> MAN (metropolitan)</br>
*Reachability from anywhere to everywhere*</br>

<u>Request for Comments</u> (RFC) are made by</br>
\- Internet Engineering TF (IETF)</br>
\- Internet Corp for Assigning Names and Numbers (ICANN)</br>
\- Internet Architecture Board (IAB)</br>

<u>Types of 'nets</u></br>
\- Intranet, internet infrastructure access\-controlled</br>
\- Extranet, parts of intranet exposed to specific non\-internal users</br>
\- Internet, public access</br>

<u>Types of Connections</u></br>
\- Cable: high bandwidth and reliability (usually TV)</br>
\- Digital Subscriber Line (DSL): internet over a telephone line</br>
\- Cellular: uses cell phone network to connect to the internet</br>
\- Satellite: phone connects to satellite, which provides connection from groundstation</br>
\- Dial\-up telephone: inexpensive, low bandwidth using a modem</br>
\- Ethernet WAN: really extentsive LAN </br>
\- Dedicated Leased Line: reserved circuits within the ISP to privately connect distant offices</br>

**\*THE CONVERGENCE\***

<u>Fault tolerant</u></br>
\- limits the number of affected devices after a failure</br>
\- has multiple paths for every data link</br>
\- provides redundancy by implementing a packet switched network</br>
**What is packet switching versus circuit switching?**</br>

<u>Scability</u></br>
\- infrastrucutre is set up to easily support more users and devices</br>
\- without impacting the services to users and purchasing a lot of new hardware</br>

<u>Quality of Service</u></br>
\- live voice and video require higher expectations, because its so obvious (and frustrating) when the quality is poor</br>
\- QoS policies establish content priority and allow the router to more easily manage flow of traffic</br>

<u>Cloud computing</u> *i hate cloud computing*</br>
\- The data is not locally stored</br>
\- Outsourcing data centers</br>
\- Cheaper</br>
\- Reliant on the internet to access</br>

## Security ##

<u>Network Security</u></br>
\- physical security (no implants)</br>
\- information security</br>
confidentiality (only intended recipients can read data)</br>
integrity (information comes out as it is sent)</br>
availibility (available when promised)</br>

<u>External threats</u></br>
\- Ransomware</br>
\- Zero\-days</br>
\- DDoS</br>
\- Data theft</br>
\- Identity theft</br>

<u>Internal Threats</u></br>
\- Lost or stolen devices</br>
\- Accidental employee misuse</br>
\- Intentional employee misuse</br>

<u>Security Solutions</u></br>
\- **Firewall**</br>
\- Access control lists</br>
\- VPNs</br>
