
# LECTURE 1: EXPLORING THE NETWORK #

<u>Peer to peer network</u>  
\- not centralized, not scalable, slow  
\- print something, very small (home) office setup

<u>Devices</u>  
\- host/server, returns and holds information  
\- clinet/end device,  makes requests  
\- intermediary, management and error information

<u>Wires</u>  
\- Metal wires, use electrical impulses  
\- Fiber optic, physical pulses of light (faster)  
\- modulation of specific EM frequencies

Network Interface Card: Connects a device to the network  
Port/Interface: A specific endpoint within a device

PAN (personal) \-\-> **LAN (local) \-\-> WAN (wide, provided by ISPs)** \-\-> MAN (metropolitan)  
\- Reachability from anywhere to everywhere

<u>Request for Comments</u> (RFC) are made by  
\- Internet Engineering TF (IETF)  
\- Internet Corp for Assigning Names and Numbers (ICANN)  
\- Internet Architecture Board (IAB)

<u>Types of 'nets</u>  
\- Intranet, internet infrastructure access\-controlled  
\- Extranet, parts of intranet exposed to specific non\-internal users  
\- Internet, public access

<u>Types of Connections</u>  
\- Cable: high bandwidth and reliability (usually TV)  
\- Digital Subscriber Line (DSL): internet over a telephone line  
\- Cellular: uses cell phone network to connect to the internet  
\- Satellite: phone connects to satellite, which provides connection from groundstation  
\- Dial\-up telephone: inexpensive, low bandwidth using a modem  
\- Ethernet WAN: really extentsive LAN  
\- Dedicated Leased Line: reserved circuits within the ISP to privately connect distant offices

<u>Fault tolerant</u>  
\- limits the number of affected devices after a failure  
\- has multiple paths for every data link  
\- provides redundancy by implementing a packet switched network  
**What is packet switching versus circuit switching?**

<u>Scability</u>  
\- infrastrucutre is set up to easily support more users and devices  
\- without impacting the services to users and purchasing a lot of new hardware

<u>Quality of Service</u>  
\- live voice and video require higher expectations, because its so obvious (and frustrating) when the quality is poor  
\- QoS policies establish content priority and allow the router to more easily manage flow of traffic

<u>Cloud computing</u> *i hate cloud computing*  
\- The data is not locally stored  
\- Outsourcing data centers  
\- Cheaper  
\- Reliant on the internet to access

## Security ##

<u>Network Security</u>  
\- physical security (no implants)  
\- information security  
confidentiality (only intended recipients can read data)  
integrity (information comes out as it is sent)  
availibility (available when promised)

<u>External threats</u>  
\- Ransomware  
\- Zero\-days  
\- DDoS  
\- Data theft  
\- Identity theft

<u>Internal Threats</u>  
\- Lost or stolen devices  
\- Accidental employee misuse  
\- Intentional employee misuse

<u>Security Solutions</u>  
\- **Firewall**  
\- Access control lists  
\- VPNs
